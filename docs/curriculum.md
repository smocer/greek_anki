# Learning guide

## Words

Seven broad groups cover nouns, names and places, verbs, pronouns, adjectives/adverbs, connecting words/prepositions and numbers. Themes such as family, food, places and classroom are optional filters within a group.

**Names & places** contains personal names, countries, cities, continents, languages and nationalities. These do not appear in the general noun collection. The word γλώσσα (“language”) is a common noun and stays in **Nouns**. Names still appear where needed in phrases and grammar exercises.

Noun cards ask for the base word: “Book” → βιβλίο. Both βιβλίο and το βιβλίο are accepted. The revealed answer shows το βιβλίο and explains its gender. Cases and plurals are practised separately.

Verb cards teach first-person present forms, such as μένω, διαβάζω, καταλαβαίνω and λείπω. These mean “I live”, “I read”, “I understand” and “I am absent”; they are not infinitives.

Pronouns include personal and demonstrative forms, question words and all seven forms μου, σου, του, της, μας, σας, τους. Each word card combines its taught meanings, such as “my” and “to me” for μου. The same forms also appear in possession and likes practice, where the full sentence identifies the intended meaning.

Numbers combine units, teens, tens, hundreds up to 900, 1000 and selected compound numbers in one group. Practice distinguishes εκατό (100) from εκατόν in numbers such as εκατόν δέκα (110).

## Phrases

- Social conversations: greetings, introductions and small talk.
- Classroom: asking, understanding and discussing lessons.
- Everyday situations: home, work and phone conversations.

Hobbies and preferences include music, drawing, swimming, playing guitar, taking photographs, watching TV and taking a break. Related vocabulary covers subjects such as history and philosophy, food and drinks, and the arithmetic signs συν, πλην, επί and διά. Paris, London and New York are in **Names & places**.

Recent vocabulary includes δουλειά, γραφείο, μητέρα, τραγούδι, μπίρα, καφές, κινητό, σταθερό, νούμερο, λείπω, λάθος, σωστό, ξανά and μόνο. Useful phrases include Πες μου, Τι είναι αυτό; and Έχεις το τηλέφωνό μου;

Work and travel vocabulary includes professions, ζωή, ζώο, ταξίδι, ταμείο, νομίζω, ζω, ψάχνω, γνωρίζω, ταξιδεύω and μαγειρεύω. Practice covers asking about work, looking for a job and describing where you work. Alternative gender forms and synonymous profession names share a card.

Family vocabulary covers parents, siblings, children, grandparents, grandchildren, cousins, aunts, uncles, nieces, nephews and relatives by marriage. Context distinguishes άντρας as man or husband, γυναίκα as woman or wife, and φίλος / φίλη as friends or romantic partners. Separate prompts identify the intended meaning. Related words include φωτογραφία, καφενείο and φυτό.

Relationship phrases practise παντρεμένος / παντρεμένη, ελεύθερος / ελεύθερη and χωρισμένος / χωρισμένη, asking about children, and introducing relatives. Naming practice contrasts Πώς τον λένε; / Πώς την λένε; with possessive του / της. The **Family** filter brings relevant words and exercises together, including previously learned μητέρα and παιδί.

## Grammar

Eight groups organise practice by skill:

- **Articles and gender:** twelve familiar nouns in “This is…” sentences, masculine/feminine relationship-status forms, and όλοι / όλες / όλα. Agreement follows the Greek noun, even when its Russian translation has a different gender.
- **Noun forms and cases:** subjects, objects, possession, plurals and forms of address; names after τον / την λένε and the accusative after από / σε / με / για. Practise ο σταθμός → τον σταθμό and replacing objects with τον / την / το, including negative replies such as Όχι, δεν τον ξέρω. Contrast Μου αρέσει ο καφές with Προτιμώ τον καφέ.
- **Verb forms:** six persons of μένω, διαβάζω and καταλαβαίνω, alongside the distinct patterns είμαι, λέγομαι and τραγουδώ. Likes practice covers all seven short pronouns with αρέσει, plural αρέσουν, Μου αρέσεις and activities with να. The verb after να agrees with the person doing the activity: Μας αρέσει να κολυμπάμε.
- **B1 verb forms:** μιλάω and αγαπάω in all six persons, accepting contracted alternatives such as μιλώ and αγαπώ.
- **B2 verb forms:** μπορώ in all six persons, contrasting endings such as -είς / -εί with B1 -άς / -άει. B1 and B2 here name conjugation classes, not language proficiency levels.
- **Location and origin:** σε with articles, από, residence and addresses.
- **Possession:** short phrases and complete sentences with μου / σου / του / της / μας / σας / τους. Distinguish the gender of the thing from the owner: Αυτή είναι η τσάντα του. Practise the additional accent in το τηλέφωνό του, το νούμερό της and η οικογένειά μου.
- **Questions and negation:** asking for information and forming negative statements; ποιος (who does the action) versus ποιον (whom it affects), and πότε in a question versus όταν joining clauses.

In hard mode the complete prompt is in English or Russian. Type its Greek translation, without Greek hints or blanks. Accents remain required; explanations appear after checking. Final-ν alternation is not a separate drill.

Alphabet, sound and handwriting practice and grammar tables are available under **Reference**.

Language practice covers asking which languages someone speaks and describing ability, from πολύ καλά to καθόλου. Nationalities share one vocabulary card for their masculine and feminine forms; language names are separate words. Find both in **Names & places**, alongside country and continent names.
