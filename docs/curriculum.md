## How Russian explanations are used

- **Ты/вы:** εσύ/εσείς and the corresponding verb forms; polite singular and plural share the same form, as in Russian.
- **Cases:** compare subject, object and possession, but avoid one-to-one assumptions. Greek από takes the accusative in these origin phrases; Russian «из России» uses the genitive.
- **Articles:** Greek marks gender/case with articles, which Russian lacks. Grammatical gender can differ: η Κύπρος is feminine, ο Καναδάς masculine, το Ιράκ neuter.
- **The present “to be”:** Greek normally retains είμαι/είσαι/είναι where Russian often omits «есть».
- **Possession and objects:** μου/σου/σας are taught in context, separately from εγώ/εσύ/εσείς. Compare Με λένε… with «Меня зовут…».
- **Naming:** Με λένε Γιώργο uses an accusative name; Λέγομαι Γιώργος and Είμαι ο Γιώργος use the nominative. Russian usually keeps the name nominative in «меня зовут Александр», so this similarity has a clear limit.
- **Direct address:** Greek preserves a productive vocative. Familiar Russian forms such as «Саш!» are a useful comparison, but are not an identical grammatical system.
- **Genuine familiar words/names:** βιβλίο and «библиотека», αριθμός and «арифметика», and Πέτρος/Пётр, Αλέξανδρος/Александр, Κωνσταντίνος/Константин. Similarities are used only where meaningful.
- Both English and Russian explanations and approximate pronunciation aids remain available. Cyrillic is a learning aid, not an exact representation of Greek sounds.

## Corrections and limits

- 7 remains **εφτά** first, **επτά** second; the same preference is used in 17.
- **σας** is not the ordinary standalone subject “you.” Use **εσείς** as the subject; σας has object/possessive functions.
- **από τη Ρωσία** is the normal spelling before ρ; the learner form **από την Ρωσία** is also accepted. Compare **από την Ελλάδα**, **από την Κύπρο**, **από το Ιράκ**.
- The vocative of a name is not determined by syllable count alone. Learn the actual examples and their patterns.
- **Τα λέμε αύριο** is naturally “See you tomorrow” / «Увидимся завтра», not a literal word-by-word translation.
- Verb “dictionary forms” such as πίνω and τραγουδώ already mean “I drink / I sing.” Modern Greek has no ordinary infinitive equivalent used like the Russian dictionary infinitive.
- Added items fill nearby gaps (greetings, negative replies, missing persons, country articles, classroom requests, 15–20); this is not intended as a complete A1 course.

## Using the expanded app

Select **Choose a topic / Выбрать тему**, search by topic or word, then choose flashcards or hard mode. **Browse / Все слова** opens the reference with examples and explanations. Cards show grammar notes only after the answer is revealed or checked.

Hard mode requires stress marks in the correct positions, along with the correct Greek letters, words, articles, and endings. Capitalization and sentence punctuation are ignored. Topic search still accepts queries without stress marks. Listed variants and explicitly allowed subject pronouns are accepted.
