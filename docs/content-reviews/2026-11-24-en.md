# English vocabulary audit — 24 November 2026

Reviewed the full source catalog as a native English editor and Modern Greek teacher: 2,062 raw card definitions across the base dictionary and all 13 additions files. This includes legacy exercises, active word cards, phrases, grammar exercises, and all 33 entries with sense arrays. Repeated definitions were reviewed for consistency rather than counted as distinct vocabulary. The source was read through a compact TOON table.

The initial review contains 140 English-field replacements across 137 card definitions. It changes explanations, one existing technical-word context example, and four sense entries. Shared repository files were not edited. The parent's removed cloze examples and diminutive normalisation are intentionally excluded. Straightforward concrete nouns with clear translations were left concise; an explanation was not added solely to fill an empty field.

## Findings and changes

- Corrected the reversed husband/man and wife/woman English sense indices so each sense aligns with Russian and Chinese.
- Removed Russian-only comparisons from English, including notebook, house, exercise, personal names, polite address and married-status explanations.
- Corrected an article-order error: η precedes γυναίκα; του follows it.
- Removed references to unseen notebook exercises and named people absent from their card.
- Explained subject/object and strong/weak pronoun distinctions in learner terms. Εσύ is a strong subject pronoun, not a weak counterpart of εσένα. Σε and σου have different grammatical functions despite English translating both with you in many sentences.
- Explained genitive forms as recipients and possessives, how word position helps distinguish them, and why noun gender differs from a person's sex.
- Clarified Greek demonstrative + article patterns, article retention with general concepts, grammatical gender for objects, and extra written accents before clitic possessives.
- Explained why αρέσω reverses the familiar English like construction and why Modern Greek να constructions still need personal verb forms.
- Replaced the awkward example “I give us the book” with “She gives us the book” on the existing μας recipient card.
- Explained negative agreement: δεν + ποτέ/τίποτα/κανείς/πουθενά is one negative meaning, not cancelling negatives.
- Expanded the 13 notebook past/future exercises with viewpoint/aspect and useful contrasts, avoiding the misleading idea that an aorist must be instantaneous. Explicitly noted that κάνω has coinciding forms.
- Added specific help for coffee ordering, φροντιστήριο, Greek-person/language/adjective distinctions, impersonal πρέπει and έχει, date questions and time expressions.
- Added short, verified memory hooks to existing words: καθαρίζω/κάθαρση/catharsis; δώρο and Θεόδωρος; φιλοσοφία; τηλέφωνο/τηλεόραση; βιβλιοθήκη; λεωφορείο; Sunday, Monday and Friday. Θεός and Θεόδωρος have no standalone cards in this source snapshot, so their story is attached to the existing δώρο card. No unrelated cards were added.

“Most frequent first” is natural English for the new ordering option and needs no change.
